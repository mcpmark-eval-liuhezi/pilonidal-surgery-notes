# Pilonidal Sinus Disease: Surgical Outcomes — First-Pass Literature Notes

**Scope:** Long-term outcomes after surgery for pilonidal sinus disease (PSD), with emphasis on **recurrence** and **postoperative infection**.
**Search basis:** PubMed database search run for this write-up (advanced search, term: *pilonidal sinus disease surgical treatment outcomes recurrence postoperative infection*, date range 2020-01-01 to 2025-12-31). All citations below come directly from that search result set; nothing has been added from memory.

---

## Citation List (as returned by the search)

### 1. PMID 41018321
**Title:** Negative Pressure Wound Therapy as an Alternative to Flap Reconstruction in an Extensive 16.5 × 11 cm Pilonidal Sinus Excision: A Case Report.
**Journal:** Cureus | **Year:** 2025
**Takeaway:** Reports a 21-year-old patient with recurrent PSD who underwent wide excision leaving a massive 16.5 × 11 cm defect; negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) achieved complete epithelialization by secondary intention with documented healing at 6 months, 1 year, and 2 years, offering a less invasive alternative to flap reconstruction in selected patients with extensive excisions.

### 2. PMID 40522552
**Title:** Evaluation of laser ablation for recurrent pilonidal sinus disease: treatment success, recurrence rates, and patient outcomes.
**Journal:** Lasers in medical science | **Year:** 2025
**Takeaway:** Evaluates laser ablation specifically in **recurrent** PSD, reporting treatment success, recurrence rates, complications, and recovery outcomes, which directly addresses the group's focus on long-term recurrence after surgery.

### 3. PMID 39800555
**Title:** Limberg flap for previously recurrent complex sacrococcygeal pilonidal sinus surgeries.
**Journal:** Acta chirurgiae plasticae | **Year:** 2024
**Takeaway:** Evaluates the Limberg (rotational rhomboid) flap for complex or recurrent sacrococcygeal pilonidal disease, reporting effects on wound infection rate, seroma formation, postoperative pain relief, **recurrence rates**, return to work, cosmetic result, and patient satisfaction.

### 4. PMID 39313437
**Title:** [Clinical application of parasacral artery perforator flap in the treatment of Pilonidal Sinus Diseases].
**Journal:** Zhonghua wei chang wai ke za zhi (Chinese journal of gastrointestinal surgery) | **Year:** 2024
**Takeaway:** Reports clinical application of a parasacral artery perforator flap for treating pilonidal sinus disease, adding a flap-based surgical option to the evidence base for PSD reconstruction.

### 5. PMID 39140695
**Title:** Results of Laser-assisted Closure (SiLaC) Surgery in Pilonidal Sinus Disease: Factors Associated With Success.
**Journal:** Surgical laparoscopy, endoscopy & percutaneous techniques | **Year:** 2024
**Takeaway:** Examines the efficacy and safety of sinus laser-assisted closure (SiLaC) in a tertiary care training hospital and identifies factors associated with success and failure, contributing to the discussion of minimally invasive surgical options and their long-term outcomes.

## Summary by Outcome Focus

- **Recurrence:** PMIDs 40522552 (laser ablation for recurrent disease), 39800555 (Limberg flap in recurrent/complex cases), and 39140695 (SiLaC success factors) are the most directly relevant to the group's primary outcome of interest.
- **Postoperative infection:** PMID 39800555 explicitly reports wound infection rate and seroma formation; PMID 41018321 addresses management of an infected post-excisional wound defect.
- **Methodological spread:** The results span case report (NPWT), laser ablation, Limberg flap, parasacral artery perforator flap, and SiLaC, reflecting current debate over flap-based versus minimally invasive surgical approaches.

## Caveats

- The search returned 5 articles within the 2020–2025 window; this is a first pass and not exhaustive. A second, complementary keyword search was run to corroborate, and the result set was kept to what the search actually returned — no tangential or remembered papers have been padded into this list.
- One article (PMID 39313437) is a Chinese-language clinical report with no abstract returned by the search, so its details are limited to title and journal metadata.
