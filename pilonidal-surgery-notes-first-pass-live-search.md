## Citation List (exactly as returned by today's search)

### A. Long-term outcomes & recurrence (priority for the review)

1. **PMID 42633928** — *International wound journal*, 2026.
   **Title:** Surgical Treatment Outcomes for Primary Pilonidal Sinus Disease: A Single-Centre Retrospective Study of 2222 Patients (2006–2020).
   **Takeaway:** The anchor citation for a ≥5-year synthesis — 2,222 patients across nine techniques with minimum 5-year follow-up reported 5-year cumulative recurrence 7.9% and postoperative infection 6.9%; lowest recurrence with Karydakis flap (1.5%), Bascom cleft lift (2.3%), and Dufourmentel flap (2.4%); most recurrences occurred within the first 2 years (median time to recurrence 20 months); higher BMI independently increased recurrence risk (HR 1.05 per kg/m²).

2. **PMID 39982788** — *Diseases of the colon and rectum*, 2025.
   **Title:** Outcome After Surgical Treatment for Chronic Pilonidal Sinus Disease: A Systematic Review of Common Surgical Techniques.
   **Takeaway:** A systematic review of common surgical techniques for chronic PSD answering the core question of which operation to choose — the anchor citation for the recurrence/comparative-effectiveness section.

3. **PMID 39174833** — *Langenbeck's archives of surgery*, 2024.
   **Title:** Long-term follow-up of pilonidal sinus disease treated by radial laser surgery.
   **Takeaway:** Retrospectively reports long-term follow-up results for radial laser surgery, directly addressing the review's core interest in long-term healing and recurrence with a minimally invasive approach.

4. **PMID 37837259** — *ANZ journal of surgery*, 2024.
   **Title:** Postoperative results of modified Karydakis flap method for primary sacrococcygeal pilonidal sinus disease: short and long-term analysis.
   **Takeaway:** Provides short- and long-term postoperative results of the modified Karydakis flap, the technique with the lowest reported recurrence in PMID 42633928.

5. **PMID 35779104** — *Pediatric surgery international*, 2022.
   **Title:** Long-term results of EPSiT in children and adolescents: still the right way to go.
   **Takeaway:** Reports long-term results of a unicentric series of pediatric patients (endoscopic pilonidal sinus treatment, EPSiT) undergone over a 5-year period.

6. **PMID 34021885** — *Updates in surgery*, 2021.
   **Title:** Pediatric endoscopic pilonidal sinus treatment (PEPSiT): what we learned after a 3-year experience in the pediatric population.
   **Takeaway:** Multi-institutional 3-year series (152 patients) showing 95.4% complete healing, low recurrence (4.6%), minor Clavien grade 2 complications (~10.7%), and excellent patient satisfaction — supports endoscopic approaches in pediatric/teen patients.

### B. Postoperative infection / wound complications (secondary endpoint)

7. **PMID 41278051** — *Cureus*, 2025.
   **Title:** Evaluating Risk Factors for Surgical Site Occurrences: Infection and Wound Dehiscence Post Definitive Surgery for Sacrococcygeal Pilonidal Sinus Disease.
   **Takeaway:** Directly quantifies postoperative surgical site infections (SSIs) and wound dehiscence after definitive pilonidal sinus surgery across multiple techniques and identifies associated risk factors — the most on-point source for the infection half of the review.

8. **PMID 40956974** — *Advances in skin & wound care*, no publication date listed.
   **Title:** Comparing Square Excision and Transposition Flap Versus Modified Limberg Flap in Sacrococcygeal Pilonidal Sinus Disease Surgery: A New Technique With Fewer Wound Issues.
   **Takeaway:** Early-outcomes comparitive study of square excision plus transposition flap versus modified Limberg flap, reporting fewer wound issues with the new technique — relevant to choosing a flap approach that minimizes postoperative wound complications.

9. **PMID 39699772** — *Techniques in coloproctology*, 2024.
   **Title:** Sinus laser therapy versus Karydakis flap procedure in the management of pilonidal sinus disease: a comparative analysis of intraoperative parameters and postoperative outcome.
   **Takeaway:** Compares minimally invasive sinus laser therapy (SiLaT) against the Karydakis flap on intraoperative parameters and postoperative outcomes — directly addresses the recurrence/infection trade-off between minimally invasive and flap surgery.

### C. Minimally invasive / laser techniques (shorter horizons)

10. **PMID 40522552** — *Lasers in medical science*, 2025.
    **Title:** Evaluation of laser ablation for recurrent pilonidal sinus disease: treatment success, recurrence rates, and patient outcomes.
    **Takeaway:** Evaluates laser ablation specifically for recurrent pilonidal sinus disease, reporting treatment success, recurrence rates, complications, and recovery outcomes — directly relevant to a recurrence-focused review.

11. **PMID 40108765** — *Lasers in surgery and medicine*, 2025.
    **Title:** A Comparitive Study Between Early Outcomes of Laser and Lay-Open Technique in Management of Simple Pilonidal Sinus.
    **Takeaway:** Compares early outcomes of laser ablation versus lay-open technique for simple pilonidal sinus, relevant to technique selection and wound/infection trade-offs in straightforward disease.

12. **PMID 39174833** — *Langenbeck's archives of surgery*, 2024 (also listed above under long-term outcomes).
    **Takeaway:** Addresses the long-term follow-up gap for laser surgery, noting most published laser series have small samples and short follow-up.
