## Consolidated Citation List (as returned by the searches)

### A. From the Advanced Search (term: "pilonidal sinus disease surgical treatment outcomes recurrence", title: "pilonidal sinus", 2020 → present)

**1. PMID 41181779** — *Recurrent Umbilical Pilonidal Sinus: An Uncommon Condition Successfully Treated With Omphalectomy and Umbilicoplasty.* — **Cureus, 2025.**
Takeaway: A rare case report (33-year-old male, 6-year history of umbilical drainage) showing that complete excision with umbilicoplasty achieved uneventful recovery with **no recurrence at 1 year**, supporting definitive surgical excision with reliable long-term results for this uncommon subtype.

**2. PMID 41074563** — *Sacrococcygeal Pilonidal Sinus Disease: A Decade-In-Review of Patient-Reported Outcome Measures.* — **ANZ Journal of Surgery, 2026.**
Takeaway: A decade-in-review of SPD highlighting that while recurrence and surgical site infection (SSI) rates are well documented, **less is known about how different surgical techniques affect patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs)** — a useful anchor for a review focused on patient-centered long-term outcomes.

**3. PMID 40522552** — *Evaluation of laser ablation for recurrent pilonidal sinus disease: treatment success, recurrence rates, and patient outcomes.* — **Lasers in Medical Science, 2025.**
Takeaway: Evaluates laser ablation specifically **in recurrent PSD**, reporting treatment success, recurrence rates, complications, and recovery outcomes — directly relevant to the review's recurrence-heavy focus.

**4. PMID 40515949** — *Long term results and quality of life after primary excision followed by Limberg plasty in pilonidal sinus disease.* — **Langenbeck's Archives of Surgery, 2025.**
Takeaway: Reports **long-term results and quality of life** after primary excision + Limberg plasty, addressing both recurrence durability and the QoL burden the group aims to capture.

**5. PMID 40498166** — *Prospective nationwide audit of short-term outcomes after surgery for chronic pilonidal sinus disease in the Netherlands.* — **Techniques in Coloproctology, 2025.**
Takeaway: A **prospective nationwide audit** characterizing PSD subtypes, practice variation, and short-term surgical outcomes — valuable population-level context for the review's technique-comparison question.

**6. PMID 40407900** — *Impact of surgical case load on recurrence rates in pilonidal sinus disease: a cross-study data synthesis.* — **International Journal of Colorectal Disease, 2025.**
Takeaway: Examines whether **annual PSD surgery volumes are associated with recurrence rates**, an under-studied system-level factor with direct relevance to where referral and outcomes decisions should be made.

### B. From the Keyword Search (cross-check / supplementary records, 2020 → present)

**7. PMID 42633928** — *Surgical Treatment Outcomes for Primary Pilonidal Sinus Disease: A Single-Centre Retrospective Study of 2222 Patients (2006–2020).* — **International Wound Journal, 2026.**
Takeaway: The strongest anchor citation for this review — a **2,222-patient single-centre cohort** (min 5-year follow-up) comparing **nine surgical techniques** on long-term recurrence and postoperative infection: 5-year cumulative recurrence 7.9%, postoperative infection 6.9%; lowest recurrence with **Karydakis flap (1.5%), Bascom cleft lift (2.3%), and Dufourmentel flap (2.4%)**; most recurrences within the first 2 years (median time to recurrence 20 months); Karydakis (HR 0.19), Bascom cleft lift (HR 0.25), Limberg (HR 0.32), and Dufourmentel (HR 0.26) were independently protective vs. lay-open.

**8. PMID 41018321** — *Negative Pressure Wound Therapy as an Alternative to Flap Reconstruction in an Extensive 16.5 × 11 cm Pilonidal Sinus Excision: A Case Report.* — **Cureus, 2025.**
Takeaway: A case report showing NPWT achieved **complete epithelialization by secondary intention with sustained healing and no recurrence at 2 years**, positioning NPWT as a less-invasive option for large post-excisional defects in selected patients.

**9. PMID 40108765** — *A Comparative Study Between Early Outcomes of Laser and Lay-Open Technique in Management of Simple Pilonidal Sinus.* — **Lasers in Surgery and Medicine, 2025.**
Takeaway: Compares **early outcomes of laser ablation vs. lay-open** for simple PSD, relevant to the minimally-invasive branch of the technique question.

**10. PMID 39174833** — *Long-term follow-up of pilonidal sinus disease treated by radial laser surgery.* — **Langenbeck's Archives of Surgery, 2024.**
Takeaway: Addresses the **long-term follow-up gap** for laser surgery, which the authors note is otherwise characterized by small samples and short follow-up — this study reports retrospective long-term results for radial laser treatment.

**11. PMID 37837259** — *Postoperative results of modified Karydakis flap method for primary sacrococcygeal pilonidal sinus disease: short and long-term analysis.* — **ANZ Journal of Surgery, 2024.**
Takeaway: Provides **short- and long-term postoperative results** of the modified Karydakis flap, the technique with the lowest reported recurrence in PMID 42633928.

**12. PMID 34021885** — *Pediatric endoscopic pilonidal sinus treatment (PEPSiT): what we learned after a 3-year experience in the pediatric population.* — **Updates in Surgery, 2021.**
Takeaway: A multi-institutional 3-year series (152 patients, median follow-up 12.8 months) showing **95.4% complete healing, low recurrence (4.6%), and minor Clavien grade 2 complications (10.7%)**, supporting endoscopic approaches in pediatric/teen patients.

## Synthesis Notes for the Review

- **Flap-based techniques dominate the long-term evidence:** PMID 42633928 (2,222 patients, ≥5-year follow-up) and PMID 37837259 both point to Karydakis-type flaps as having the lowest independent recurrence risk; Limberg and Dufourmentel flaps also performed well.
- **Minimally invasive options are growing but evidence is shorter:** laser ablation (PMIDs 40522552, 40108765, 39174833) and endoscopic PEPSiT (PMID 34021885) show favorable short- and medium-term results, but ≥5-year recurrence data remain limited — a documented gap the review should flag.
- **Infection is consistently reported alongside recurrence:** PMIDs 40498166 (nationwide audit), 40407900 (caseload synthesis), and 40515949 (QoL/Limberg long-term) all contextualize infection and recovery within broader outcome profiling.
- **System-level factor:** PMID 40407900's caseload-volume analysis is a near-unique contribution for a surgical outcomes review.
- **Honest limitation:** Several records carry 2026 publication dates as returned by the live search; several minimally invasive series report medium-term rather than ≥5-year recurrence. The long-term anchor for the ≥5-year synthesis remains **PMID 42633928** (2,222-patient, ≥5-year cohort).
