# Pilonidal Sinus Disease — Surgical Outcomes: Literature Notes (First Pass)

**Topic:** Long-term outcomes after surgery for pilonidal sinus disease (PSD), with emphasis on **recurrence** and **postoperative infection**.

**Prepared for:** Surgery research group — first-pass literature review.

## Citation list (as returned by the search)

### 1. PMID 42717603
- **Title:** Comparison of Phenol Application and Endoscopic Pilonidal Sinus Treatment in Pediatric Pilonidal Sinus Disease.
- **Authors:** Çolak, İsbir, Kıllı, Taşan, Taşkınlar, Naycı
- **Journal:** Pediatrics International (official journal of the Japan Pediatric Society)
- **Year:** 2026
- **Takeaway:** Compares two minimally invasive approaches — phenol application vs. endoscopic pilonidal sinus treatment (EPSiT) — in children, contributing pediatric comparative-outcome data to the increasingly minimally invasive treatment landscape.

### 2. PMID 42703471
- **Title:** Surgical Management of Pilonidal Sinus: A Comparative Study of Excision, Limberg Flap, and Keystone Flap Reconstruction.
- **Authors:** Punyo, Yadav, Krishna, Maurya, Jain, Jain
- **Journal:** Cureus
- **Year:** 2026
- **Takeaway:** In a prospective comparative study of 46 patients, off-midline flap reconstruction (Limberg, Keystone) offered superior long-term outcomes — no recurrence in flap groups vs. 13.3% recurrence with simple excision — at the cost of longer operative time and higher seroma-related complication rates.

### 3. PMID 42700095
- **Title:** Comparative outcomes of phenol therapy and Karydakis flap in pilonidal sinus disease: A retrospective cohort study with 1-year follow-up.
- **Authors:** Osmanov, Hazir, Ekici
- **Journal:** Medicine
- **Year:** 2026
- **Takeaway:** In 125 patients, phenol therapy achieved significantly faster wound healing and return to work than the Karydakis flap, with no statistically significant difference in 1-year recurrence (12.5% vs. 9.1%), supporting phenol as a minimally invasive option for selected primary PSD.

### 4. PMID 42633928
- **Title:** Surgical Treatment Outcomes for Primary Pilonidal Sinus Disease: A Single-Centre Retrospective Study of 2222 Patients (2006-2020).
- **Authors:** Tokocin, Yigitbas, Yavuz, Gulcicek
- **Journal:** International Wound Journal
- **Year:** 2026
- **Takeaway:** The most directly relevant hit for this review — a large single-centre cohort (n = 2222, minimum 5-year follow-up) comparing nine surgical techniques, reporting 5-year cumulative recurrence of 7.9% and postoperative infection of 6.9%, with the lowest recurrence for Karydakis flap (1.5%), Bascom cleft lift (2.3%), and Dufourmentel flap (2.4%), and most recurrences occurring within the first 2 years (median 20 months).

### 5. PMID 42593573
- **Title:** Outcomes of minimally invasive pit-picking treatment in adolescents with uncomplicated pilonidal disease: a retrospective single-center study.
- **Authors:** Başar, Erginel, Kocaman, Keskin, Gün Soysal
- **Journal:** Pediatric Surgery International
- **Year:** 2026
- **Takeaway:** A retrospective single-center study evaluating pit-picking outcomes in adolescents with uncomplicated disease, adding adolescent-specific data on morbidity and recurrence for a minimally invasive technique.

### 6. PMID 42563349
- **Title:** Simulation-Based Training in the Limberg Flap Technique for Pilonidal Sinus Disease.
- **Authors:** Nafees, Aftab, Faheem, Shahid, Ali
- **Journal:** Journal of the College of Physicians and Surgeons--Pakistan (JCPSP)
- **Year:** 2026
- **Takeaway:** Evaluates simulation-based training for the Limberg flap technique on real patients — relevant mainly as education/surgical-training context; **limited direct contribution to long-term outcome evidence**, so flag as peripheral to this review.

### 7. PMID 42560577
- **Title:** Laser-assisted endoscopic treatment of pilonidal sinus disease in children: a first pediatric series.
- **Authors:** Türker Çolak, Bal, Tutuş, Kılıç, Özden, Tuncer
- **Journal:** Pediatric Surgery International
- **Year:** 2026
- **Takeaway:** First pediatric series of laser-assisted endoscopic pilonidal sinus treatment (LEPSIT), describing technical details and early-stage clinical outcomes only — **early-phase technique report, not long-term outcome data**, so also flag as peripheral for this review.

## Notes for the group

- **Core long-term outcome evidence from this pass** rests mainly on PMID 42633928 (large cohort, 5-year recurrence + infection by technique) and, secondarily, PMID 42703471 and 42700095 (comparative studies with 1-year and short-term follow-up).
- The remaining hits are pediatric or technique-focused with short/early follow-up, or training-related; they are included here for completeness because the search returned them, but they contribute little to the long-term recurrence/infection question.
- **Next steps suggested:** a second, broader search pass (e.g., "pilonidal" AND recurrence; specific technique names such as Karydakis, Bascom cleft lift, Limberg, EPSiT, laser), no date limit or wider date range, plus targeted searches for systematic reviews/meta-analyses and registries, would strengthen the pool of long-term outcome studies.
