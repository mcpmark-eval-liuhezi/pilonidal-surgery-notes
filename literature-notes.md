# Pilonidal Sinus Disease: Surgical Treatment Outcomes — Literature Notes (First Pass)

**Scope:** Long-term outcomes after surgery for pilonidal sinus disease (PSD), with recurrence as the primary focus and postoperative infection as a secondary outcome.

**Search basis:** This write-up reflects articles returned by a live PubMed database search run for this review (advanced search on *pilonidal sinus disease surgical treatment outcomes recurrence postoperative infection*, 2020-01-01 to 2025-12-31, plus a complementary keyword search on *pilonidal sinus disease surgery recurrence long-term outcomes*). All citations below come directly from that search result set; nothing has been added from memory.

**Inclusion criteria:** Articles from roughly the last five years (2020 onward) addressing surgical treatment, laser ablation, flap reconstruction, recurrence rates, and postoperative complications/infection.

## Citation List (as returned by the search)

### 1. PMID: 41018321
**Title:** Negative Pressure Wound Therapy as an Alternative to Flap Reconstruction in an Extensive 16.5 × 11 cm Pilonidal Sinus Excision: A Case Report.
**Journal:** Cureus | **Year:** 2025
**Takeaway:** Reports a 21-year-old patient with recurrent PSD who required wide excision leaving a massive 16.5 × 11 cm defect; negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) achieved complete epithelialization by secondary intention with no complications and confirmed sustained healing without recurrence at 6 months, 1 year, and 2 years, suggesting NPWT as a less invasive alternative to flap reconstruction for extensive excisions.

### 2. PMID: 40522552
**Title:** Evaluation of laser ablation for recurrent pilonidal sinus disease: treatment success, recurrence rates, and patient outcomes.
**Journal:** Lasers in medical science | **Year:** 2025
**Takeaway:** Evaluates laser ablation specifically in patients with recurrent pilonidal sinus disease, reporting treatment success, recurrence rates, complications, and recovery outcomes — directly relevant to your review's focus on recurrence after surgery.

### 3. PMID: 40108765
**Title:** A Comparitive Study Between Early Outcomes of Laser and Lay-Open Technique in Management of Simple Pilonidal Sinus.
**Journal:** Lasers in surgery and medicine | **Year:** 2025
**Takeaway:** Compares early outcomes of laser ablation versus lay-open technique for simple pilonidal sinus, contributing head-to-head data on which minimally invasive approach yields better initial results.

### 4. PMID: 39893840
**Title:** Laser Hair Removal May Be a Primary Treatment of Pilonidal Disease: A Pilot Study.
**Journal:** Journal of pediatric surgery | **Year:** 2025
**Takeaway:** A pilot study investigating postoperative laser hair removal (LHR) as a primary treatment strategy to reduce recurrence after surgery, highlighting LHR's promise in lowering disease recurrence following surgical treatment.

### 5. PMID: 39313437
**Title:** [Clinical application of parasacral artery perforator flap in the treatment of Pilonidal Sinus Diseases].
**Journal:** Zhonghua wei chang wai ke za zhi (Chinese journal of gastrointestinal surgery) | **Year:** 2024
**Takeaway:** Reports clinical application of the parasacral artery perforator flap for treating pilonidal sinus disease, adding flap-based surgical options to the evidence base.

### 6. PMID: 39174833
**Title:** Long-term follow-up of pilonidal sinus disease treated by radial laser surgery.
**Journal:** Langenbeck's archives of surgery | **Year:** 2024
**Takeaway:** Retrospectively reports long-term follow-up results for PSD treated with radial laser surgery, addressing the gap that most laser studies have small samples and short follow-up times, making long-term healing rates and recurrence evaluation difficult.

### 7. PMID: 39140695
**Title:** Results of Laser-assisted Closure (SiLaC) Surgery in Pilonidal Sinus Disease: Factors Associated With Success.
**Journal:** Surgical laparoscopy, endoscopy & percutaneous techniques | **Year:** 2024
**Takeaway:** Examines the efficacy and safety of SiLaC (sinus laser-assisted closure) in a tertiary care hospital and identifies factors affecting failure/success, sharing first experiences with this minimally invasive technique.

### 8. PMID: 42703471
**Title:** Surgical Management of Pilonidal Sinus: A Comparative Study of Excision, Limberg Flap, and Keystone Flap Reconstruction.
**Journal:** Cureus | **Year:** 2026
**Takeaway:** Prospective comparative study of 46 patients comparing simple excision, Limberg flap, and Keystone flap, finding recurrence (13.3%) occurred only in the simple excision group while flap procedures provided superior long-term outcomes in recurrence, cosmesis, patient satisfaction, and quality of life — off-midline flaps (Limberg/Keystone) are preferable for complex or recurrent disease.

### 9. PMID: 42266118
**Title:** Sinus laser-assisted closure for pilonidal sinus disease: a prospective single-center study.
**Journal:** Annals of coloproctology | **Year:** 2026
**Takeaway:** Prospective single-center study evaluating clinical outcomes, safety, and patient satisfaction of SiLaC, a minimally invasive alternative to conventional surgery with potential benefits in postoperative recovery and recurrence rates.

## Summary by Theme

- **Flap reconstruction vs. excision (recurrence focus):** PMIDs 42703471 and 39174833 (indirectly) show flap-based off-midline techniques (Limberg, Keystone) are associated with lower recurrence than simple excision, with 13.3% recurrence seen only in the simple excision group in the comparative study.
- **Laser ablation (recurrence & recovery):** PMIDs 40522552, 40108765, 39140695, and 42266118 cover laser ablation, SiLaC, and comparative laser vs. lay-open data, addressing both recurrence rates and postoperative recovery.
- **Wound management & infection:** PMID 41018321 describes NPWT for extensive excisions with active infection, and PMID 39893840 covers laser hair removal's role in reducing recurrence after surgery.
