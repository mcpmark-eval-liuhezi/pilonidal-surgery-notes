# Pilonidal Sinus Disease: Surgical Outcomes — First-Pass Literature Notes

**Review focus:** long-term outcomes after surgery for pilonidal sinus disease (PSD) — **recurrence above all**, with **postoperative infection** as the secondary endpoint.

**Provenance (hard requirement):** every citation below comes from a **PubMed database search run now** (live search calls made for this pass, 2020-01-01 → present / ~last five years). Nothing is recalled from memory. Tangential items (e.g., a verrucous carcinoma case report, an Ayurveda case report) were deliberately excluded rather than padded in.

## Citation list (as returned by the search)

### A. Long-term outcomes & recurrence (highest priority for the review)

1. **PMID 42633928** — *International wound journal*, 2026.
   **"Surgical Treatment Outcomes for Primary Pilonidal Sinus Disease: A Single-Centre Retrospective Study of 2222 Patients (2006–2020)."**
   Takeaway: This is the anchor citation for the ≥5-year synthesis — 2,222 patients across nine techniques with a minimum 5-year follow-up reported 5-year cumulative recurrence of 7.9% and postoperative infection of 6.9%, with the lowest recurrence for Karydakis flap (1.5%), Bascom cleft lift (2.3%), and Dufourmentel flap (2.4%), most recurrences within the first 2 years (median time to recurrence 20 months), and higher BMI as an independent risk factor (HR 1.05 per kg/m²).

2. **PMID 41854814** — *Updates in Surgery*, 2026.
   **"Comparative study of the Karydakis flap versus conventional surgical techniques for pilonidal sinus disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis."**
   Takeaway: A systematic review and meta-analysis of 15 studies (n = 3,108, through February 2025) finding the Karydakis flap significantly reduces recurrence versus midline closure (OR 0.30, 95% CI 0.16–0.59; p < 0.001) and infection, with seroma, operative time, hospital stay, complications, and return-to-work as secondary outcomes.

3. **PMID 41838186** — *Techniques in coloproctology*, 2026.
   **"Surgical preference and outcomes in pilonidal sinus disease: a national multicenter study evaluating complications, recurrence, and quality of life (PISI TURKEY)."**
   Takeaway: A national Turkish multicenter study integrating real-world complications, recurrence, and quality-of-life data comparing excision-based versus non-excisional (minimally invasive) procedures — useful for，发生 practice patterns and QoL weighting in the review.

4. **PMID 42098544** — *Techniques in coloproctology*, 2026.
   **"Comparing keystone and Limberg flaps for pilonidal sinus: Does flap choice influence patient satisfaction?"**
   Takeaway: A randomized comparative study of keystone perforator island flap vs. Limberg flap, primary outcome patient satisfaction by flap type, with perioperative outcomes as secondary endpoints.

5. **PMID 42703471** — *Cureus*, 2026.
   **"Surgical Management of Pilonidal Sinus: A Comparative Study of Excision, Limberg Flap, and Keystone Flap Reconstruction."**
   Takeaway: Prospective comparative study of 46 patients showing recurrence (13.3%) occurred **only** in the simple excision group (p < 0.001), with flaps offering superior scar quality, satisfaction, and quality of life at the cost of longer operative time and stay.

6. **PMID 42311045** — *World journal of surgery*, 2026.
   **"Karydakis Versus Limberg Flap in Primary Sacrococcygeal Pilonidal Sinus: A Retrospective Comparative Study of Functional Recovery."**
   Takeaway: Compares patient-reported recovery (sexual function and driving ability) between Karydakis and Limberg flaps in young, active patients — directly relevant to the functional-restoration angle of long-term outcomes.

7. **PMID 41752637** — *Medicina (Kaunas)*, 2026.
   **"The Use of Methylene Blue in Pilonidal Sinus Surgery Reduces the Risk of Recurrence: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis."**
   Takeaway: Systematic review/meta-analysis supporting intraoperative methylene blue use to identify sinus tracts and reduce recurrence.

### B. Minimally invasive & laser techniques (recurrence + infection in shorter horizons)

8. **PMID 42988951** — *Langenbeck's archives of surgery*, 2026.
   **"Endoscopic pilonidal sinus treatment versus Limberg flap reconstruction for uncomplicated pilonidal sinus disease: a prospective comparative study."**
   Takeaway: Prospective head-to-head comparison of minimally invasive EPSiT vs. established off-midline Limberg flap, addressing whether endoscopic treatment can match flap Rep，果然 persisting with off-midline reconstruction on recurrence.

9. **PMID 42852833** — *Scandinavian journal of surgery*, 2026.
   **"Outpatient laser treatment of pilonidal sinus disease: A retrospective study of short-term outcomes."**
   Takeaway: Evaluates short-term outcomes of outpatient SiLaC laser treatment and whether clinic-based delivery affects postoperative recovery and recurrence rates.

10. **PMID 42593573** — *Pediatric surgery international*, 2026.
    **"Outcomes of minimally invasive pit-picking treatment in adolescents with uncomplicated pilonidal disease: a retrospective single-center study."**
    Takeaway: Retrospective single-center series of pit-picking in adolescents, addressing recurrence and postoperative morbidity with reduced surgical trauma and faster recovery.

11. **PMID 42560577** — *Pediatric surgery international*, 2026.
    **"Laser-assisted endoscopic treatment of pilonidal sinus disease in children: a first pediatric series."**
    Takeaway: First pediatric series of LEPSIT describing technical details and early-stage clinical outcomes in a pediatric cohort.

12. **PMID 41901600** — *Medicina (Kaunas)*, 2026.
    **"The New Minimally Invasive Techniques for Pilonidal Sinus Disease Surgery: Choosing the Right Technique for the Right Patient — A Narrative Review."**
    Takeaway: Narrative review guiding selection among EPSiT, SiLaC, and laser techniques by patient factors (no abstract returned by the search).

13. **PMID 41078023** — *Lasers in surgery and medicine*, 2025.
    **"International Society of Laser Proctology Position Paper on SiLaC and EPSiT/SiLaC in the Management of Pilonidal Sinus Disease."**
    Takeaway: International consensus position paper on standardizing minimally invasive laser/endoscopic technique reporting and use in PSD management.

14. **PMID 40974390** — *International journal of colorectal disease*, 2025.
    **"Intra-operative ultrasound in the surgical treatment of complex and recurrent pilonidal disease: a retrospective, observational, single-center study."**
    Takeaway: Evaluates combining intraoperative ultrasound with EPSiT for complex/recurrent disease to improve tract identification and postoperative recovery.

### C. Postoperative infection & wound complications (secondary endpoint)

15. **PMID 42089833** — *International wound journal*, 2026.
    **"Outcomes of Pilonidal Surgery Performed in the Setting of Standard Adjunct Laser Hair Removal: A Retrospective Cohort Study."**
    Takeaway: Real-world US cohort (n = 168, Kaiser Permanente Northern California, 2012–2024) with uniform adjunct laser hair removal reporting 30-day SSI of 14.4% and repeat procedure rate of 16.7%, with Bascom flap closure having fewer repeat procedures than pilocystectomy.

16. **PMID 41278051** — *Cureus*, 2025.
    **"Evaluating Risk Factors for Surgical Site Occurrences: Infection and Wound Dehiscence Post Definitive Surgery for Sacrococcygeal Pilonidal Sinus Disease."**
    Takeaway: Evaluates rates of surgical site infections (SSIs) and wound dehiscence and identifies associated risk factors across multiple surgical techniques.

## Sequencing restriction (honest limitation)

Published 2026 dates are reported exactly as returned by the search. Several minimally invasive series report short- to medium-term recurrence data rather than ≥5-year follow-up; the long-term anchor for the ≥5-year synthesis remains **PMID 42633928** (2,222 patients, 5-year minimum follow-up). **Suggested follow-up pass:** long-term cohorts with explicit ≥5-year recurrence and infection endpoints, plus head-to-head laser vs. flap RCTs.
