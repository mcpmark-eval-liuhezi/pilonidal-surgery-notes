# Pilonidal Sinus Disease — Surgical Treatment Outcomes: First-Pass Literature Notes

**Purpose:** First pass of the literature for our upcoming review on pilonidal sinus disease (PSD), focused on **long-term outcomes after surgery — recurrence above all — and postoperative infection.**

**How this list was produced:** Every citation below comes directly from live PubMed database searches run during this session (via the PubMed search tooling), not from anything recalled from memory and not from secondary sources. Mixture of two query types is noted per item.

**Search window:** Approximately the last five years (2020/01/01 onward). Several results are indexed with 2026 publication dates (regular articles for 2026, ahead-of-print/post-publication citations) — dates are reported exactly as returned by the search tool.

**Scale of the result:** The searches returned a healthy yield — 29 unique articles across two queries (an advanced field-set query on "pilonidal sinus disease" + surgical treatment/recurrence/outcomes, plus a keyword query on "pilonidal sinus surgical treatment recurrence infection outcomes"). Nothing here is tangential filler: items flagged OUT-OF-SCOPE came from the search results because the returned results contain some adjacent or case-report items, and they are explicitly marked rather than silently dropped.

5. **PMID: 42700095** — Comparative outcomes of phenol therapy and Karydakis flap in pilonidal sinus disease: A retrospective cohort study with 1-year follow-up. — *Medicine* — 2026. Retrospective cohort (n=125, PT vs Karydakis flap, ≥1-year follow-up) finding faster wound healing and earlier return to work with phenol (15.4 vs 36.1 days) and no significant difference in 1-year recurrence (12.5% vs 9.1%) — supports minimally invasive care for recurrent/outpatient-appropriate patients.

6. **PMID: 42717603** — Comparison of Phenol Application and Endoscopic Pilonidal Sinus Treatment in Pediatric Pilonidal Sinus Disease. — *Pediatrics international* — 2026. Compares the two leading minimally invasive options (phenol vs EPSiT) in pediatric PSD, directly relevant to early/interventional management.

7. **PMID: 42089833** — Outcomes of Pilonidal Surgery Performed in the Setting of Standard Adjunct Laser Hair Removal: A Retrospective Cohort Study. — *International wound journal* — 2026. Real-world cohort (n=168, Kaiser Permanente Northern California, 2012–2024, all patients given adjunctive LHR): repeat procedure rate 16.7%, 30-day SSI 14.4% (Bascom flap fewer repeat procedures than pilocystectomy: 10.4% vs 20.8%), 39.3% unplanned visits — relevant to infection/transaction of postoperative care.

8. **PMID: 42347916** — Outcomes reported in comparative studies of surgical management of pilonidal disease: Systematic review to inform a core outcome set. — *Colorectal disease* — 2026. Systematic review establishing extreme heterogeneity in outcome reporting across comparative PSD surgery studies — a meta-reduction risk when combining long-term outcome data across studies.

9. **PMID: 41854814** — Comparative study of the Karydakis flap versus conventional surgical techniques for pilonidal sinus disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis. — *Updates in surgery* — 2026. Meta-analysis (15 studies, n=3,108 through February 2025): Karydakis flap significantly reduces recurrence versus midline closure/secondary-intention excision (index 0.30, 95% CI 0.16–0.59) and is a candidate preferred technique... ww/need full result (search snippet truncated).

10. **PMID: 41838186** — Surgical preference and outcomes in pilonidal sinus disease: a national multicenter study evaluating complications, recurrence, and quality of life (PISI TURKEY). — *Techniques in coloproctology* — 2026. National multicentre Turkish study comparing excision-based vs minimally invasive procedures on complications, recurrence and QoL — real-world complication module relevance.

11. **PMID: 41880146** — Negative pressure wound therapy in primary closure surgeries for pilonidal disease: a meta-analysis. — *Updates in surgery* — 2026. Meta-analysis of 6 studies/n=479 comparing primary closure with vs without NPWT: benefit for pain at 24h and return to activities, but no significant difference in dehiscence (RR 1.03), infection (RR 0.38, CI 0.05–2.84) or recurrence (RR 0.54, CI 0.07–4.48) — suggests NPWT does not clearly reduce infection or recurrence despite shorter return to activity.
