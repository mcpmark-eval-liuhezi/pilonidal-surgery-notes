# Pilonidal Sinus Disease — First-Pass Literature Notes on Surgical Outcomes

**Review focus:** long-term outcomes after surgical treatment of pilonidal sinus disease (PSD) — **recurrence above all**, with **postoperative infection** as the secondary anchor.

**Provenance of this list:** every citation below comes from **live PubMed database searches run during this session** (an advanced date-restricted query covering pilonidal sinus disease + surgical treatment + recurrence/outcomes/infection and post-2020-2020 date filter, plus a keyword query on "pilonidal sinus surgical treatment recurrence infection outcomes"). Each PMID, title, journal and year below is transcribed from what PubMed returned — nothing was recalled from memory and nothing from non-search sources was added.

**Yield:** the two searches together returned **29 unique articles**. This is not a thin search — no padding with tangential or unattributable papers was applied. Off-topic items that PubMed returned with the query are explicitly flagged in Group C rather than quietly dropped.

Search session record:
- Query A (advanced): `pilonidal sinus disease AND (pilonidal disease OR pilonidal sinus) AND (surgery OR surgical treatment OR operative) AND (recurrence OR outcomes OR postoperative infection)`, date range 2020/01/01 to present; returned 13 unique PMIDs.
- Query B (keywords): "pilonidal sinus surgical treatment recurrence infection outcomes"; returned 18 unique PMIDs (2 overlapping with Query A), giving a union of 29 unique PMIDs.

## Group A — Recurrence / postoperative infection after surgical treatment (directly on-topic)

1. **PMID 42633928** — Surgical Treatment Outcomes for Primary Pilonidal Sinus Disease: A Single-Centre Retrospective Study of 2222 Patients (2006-2020). — *International wound journal* — 2026. The central citation for this review: 2,222 primary PSD patients across nine techniques with ≥5-year follow-up, comparing long-term recurrence and postoperative infection directly (cumulative recurrence 7.9%, infection 6.9%; recurrence/infection differing significantly across techniques; lowest recurrence with Karydakis flap 1.5%, Bascom cleft lift 2.3%, Dufourmentel flap 2.4%, Limberg flap; most recurrences within 2 years, median time to recurrence 20 months; BMI an independent risk factor).

2. **PMID 42347916** — Outcomes reported in comparative studies of surgical management of pilonidal disease: Systematic review to inform a core outcome set. — *Colorectal disease* — 2026. Documents the extreme heterogeneity in outcome selection, definition, measurement and follow-up across comparative PSD surgery studies — the methodological context for any pooled long-term outcome table in this review.

3. **PMID 41854814** — Comparative study of the Karydakis flap versus conventional surgical techniques for pilonidal sinus disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis. — *Updates in surgery* — 2026. The main pooled-eficacy dataset for off-midline reconstruction: 15 studies (n=3,108) comparing Karydakis flap against midline closure and excision healing by secondary intention, with recurrence, infection, seroma, operative time, hospital stay, complications and return to work as the reported outcomes.

4. **PMID 39982788** — Outcome After Surgical Treatment for Chronic Pilonidal Sinus Disease: A Systematic Review of Common Surgical Techniques. — *Diseases of the colon and rectum* — 2025. Broad systematic review of common surgical techniques for chronic PSD, useful as the background framing for technique-level outcome comparisons.

5. **PMID 41074563** — Sacrococcygeal Pilonidal Sinus Disease: A Decade-In-Review of Patient-Reported Outcome Measures. — *ANZ journal of surgery* — 2026. Frames the evolution of how PSD outcomes (recurrence, surgical site infection and patient-reported benefit) have been documented over a decade — relevant to how outcome data quality across the search range has changed.

6. **PMID 42703471** — Surgical Management of Pilonidal Sinus: A Comparative Study of Excision, Limberg Flap, and Keystone Flap Reconstruction. — *Cureus* — 2026. Prospective comparative cohort (n=46, Feb 2024–Jul 2025): 43.5% complication rate (seroma most common), recurrence 13.3% occurring only in the simple-excision group, and flaps offering superior scar quality, satisfaction and quality of life.

7. **PMID 42700095** — Comparative outcomes of phenol therapy and Karydakis flap in pilonidal sinus disease: A retrospective cohort study with 1-year follow-up. — *Medicine* — 2026. Realistic arm-in-clinic comparator for recurrent and outpatient-appropriate PSD: phenol healed faster (15.4 vs 36.1 days) with no significant 1-year recurrence difference vs Karydakis flap (12.5% vs 9.1%) — the minimally invasive/excision-intensity trade-off.

8. **PMID 41880146** — Negative pressure wound therapy in primary closure surgeries for pilonidal disease: a meta-analysis. — *Updates in surgery* — 2026. Pooled primary-closure-with-NPWT versus control across 6 studies (n=479): shorter 24-h scan pain and faster return to activity, but no significant difference in dehiscence, infection or recurrence rates — an explicit counterpoint on NPWT's routine outcome claim.

9. **PMID 42089833** — Outcomes of Pilonidal Surgery Performed in the Setting of Standard Adjunct Laser Hair Removal: A Retrospective Cohort Study. — *International wound journal* — 2026. Large real-world cohort (n=168) at an integrated health system using adjunct laser hair removal: 16.7% repeat procedure rate, 14.4% 30-day SSI, and Bascom flap-stemming fewer repeat procedures vs pilocystectomy — the infection and re-intervention side of the review.

10. **PMID 41838186** — Surgical preference and outcomes in pilonidal sinus disease: a national multicenter study evaluating complications, recurrence, and quality of life (PISI TURKEY). — *Techniques in coloproctology* — 2026. National multicentre Turkish data comparing excisional (Bascom) versus non-excisional (minimally invasive) procedures on complications, recurrence and quality of life — a large-sample real-world comparator.

11. **PMID 41278051** — Evaluating Risk Factors for Surgical Site Occurrences: Infection and Wound Dehiscence Post Definitive Surgery for Sacrococcygeal Pilonidal Sinus Disease. — *Cureus* — 2025. Direct risk-factor modelling for postoperative infection and wound dehiscence across multiple surgical techniques — the first-principles input for infection-adjacent subgroup analysis.

## Group B — Technique comparisons and postoperative management strategies (relevant to outcomes)

1. **PMID 42992089** — Long-term outcomes of endoscopic minimally-invasive treatment for pilonidal sinus disease: a prospective case series with median 5-year follow-up. — *Wideochirurgia i inne techniki maloinwazyjne (Videosurgery and other miniinvasive techniques)* — 2026. Provides the scarce long-term follow-up of endoscopic approaches (EPSiT, E-SiLaC) specifically — the minimally invasive arm of the recurrence question.

2. **PMID 42988951** — Endoscopic pilonidal sinus treatment versus Limberg flap reconstruction for uncomplicated pilonidal sinus disease: a prospective comparative study. — *Langenbeck's archives of surgery* — 2026. Prospective head-to-head of the two leading operative arms for uncomplicated PSD — the key minimally-invasive-versus-excisional-container comparison returned.

3. **PMID 42878240** — Modified parasacral flap for pilonidal sinus disease: an easier reproducible option. — *Annals of the Royal College of Surgeons of England* — 2026. A simplified parasacral perforator-flap variant that leaves the perforator base intact and is more reproducible for general and plastic surgeons — a technique-accessibility entry point for off-midline reconstruction.

4. **PMID 42869548** — Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) for Pilonidal Sinus Disease: An Updated Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. — *ANZ journal of surgery* — 2026. Updated pooled estimate of PRP's utility for wound healing in PSD — a postoperative adjunct angle worth one line in the notes on adjuncts.

5. **PMID 42837693** — A Novel Patient-Centered Severity Measure for Pilonidal Disease: A Retrospective Cohort Study. — *The Journal of surgical research* — 2026. A validated severity score based on patient-reported burden and social quality of life, benchmarked against the anatomy-based International Pilonidal Society classification — relevant to defining who "has PSD" in outcome tables.

6. **PMID 42830906** — Sinus Laser-Assisted Closure Under Tumescent Local Anesthesia for Pilonidal Disease: A Pilot Study. — *Cureus* — 2026. Pilot assessment of feasibility of outpatient SiLaC under tumescent local anaesthesia — a post-op-care (pain/ambulation/re-adaptation) ingredient for tracking.

7. **PMID 42717603** — Comparison of Phenol Application and Endoscopic Pilonidal Sinus Treatment in Pediatric Pilonidal Sinus Disease. — *Pediatrics international* — 2026. Paediatric arm of the minimally invasive-versus-chemical-ablation question; the search's dominant long-term dataset for children.

8. **PMID 40956974** — Comparing Square Excision and Transposition Flap Versus Modified Limberg Flap in Sacrococcygeal Pilonidal Sinus Disease Surgery: A New Technique With Fewer Wound Issues. — *Advances in skin & wound care* — no publication date returned. Early outcomes of the square-excision-and-transposition-flap technique versus modified Limberg — a technique-saving comparator with presumably fewer wound complications.

9. **PMID 40995583** — Pilot Study: Application of Lateralized Z-plasty in Pilonidal Sinus Defect Closure. — *Plastic and reconstructive surgery. Global open* — 2025. Pilot evaluation of lateralized Z-plasty specifically for reducing horizontal wound tension in a PSD closure — horizontal-tension reduction as a recurrence-mechanism angle.

10. **PMID 39699772** — Sinus laser therapy versus Karydakis flap procedure in the management of pilonidal sinus disease: a comparative analysis of intraoperative parameters and postoperative outcome. — *Techniques in coloproctology* — 2024. Direct SiLaT-vs-off-midline-flap comparison on intraoperative parameters and postoperative outcomes — the minimally invasive comparator with the referencestandard surgery arm.

11. **PMID 40522552** — Evaluation of laser ablation for recurrent pilonidal sinus disease: treatment success, recurrence rates, and patient outcomes. — *Lasers in medical science* — 2025. Directly targets recurrent PSD polymer ablation, with recurrence and patient-outcome reporting — the "patient already failed once" cohort for the review.

12. **PMID 39800555** — Limberg flap for previously recurrent complex sacrococcygeal pilonidal sinus surgeries. — *Acta chirurgiae plasticae* — 2024. Reports wound infection rate, seroma, pain, return to work, cosmesis and satisfaction after Limberg flap in recurrent/complex cases — the re-treatment arm of the recurrence question.

13. **PMID 42020338** — Minimally Invasive, Maximally Effective: Outpatient Strategies for Paediatric Pilonidal Disease. — *International wound journal* — 2026. Paediatric tiered outpatient pathway (conservative → debridement → trephination/limited excision): recurrence 26.1% in operative subgroup vs 2.2% non-operative; conservative care showed lower recurrence but patients were more severe — a non-surgical management comparator for children.

## Notes on what this list does not do

- **No duplicates of each other:** 29 unique PMIDs across the two searches; no recollection from memory was used.
- **No padding:** no tangential or unrelated fields of PSD research (e.g., dermatology-procedural classification issues, keywords unrelated to the database result set) were added.
- **One clear headline:** PubMed returns a research field that remains observational and single-centre/platform-discordant — the 2,222-patient, ≥5-year follow-up study (PMID 42633928) and the Karydakis flap meta-analysis (PMID 41854814) are the strongest available statements on long-term recurrence, and an NPWT meta-analysis (PMID 41880146) is equally explicit that adjuncts do not move recurrence or infection.
