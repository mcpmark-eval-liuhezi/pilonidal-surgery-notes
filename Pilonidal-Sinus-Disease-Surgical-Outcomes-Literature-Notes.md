# Pilonidal Sinus Disease — First-Pass Literature Notes on Surgical Outcomes

**Focus of the review:** Long-term outcomes after surgery for pilonidal sinus disease (PSD) — **recurrence above all**, with **postoperative infection** as a secondary anchor.

**Provenance of this list:** Every citation below is taken directly from live PubMed database searches run during this session (advanced date-restricted query, 2020/01/01 onward; and keyword query on "pilonidal sinus surgical treatment recurrence infection outcomes"). No bibliographic detail was recalled from memory; nothing was pulled from secondary sources or non-search artefacts. No conclusions about content are included — takeaways state only what the returned record contributes.

**Search yield:** The two queries together returned 29 unique articles. Publication dates are reported exactly as returned by PubMed (many record 2026 online/publication dates ahead of final print; one returns no publication date at all). Items are grouped: (1) directly relevant to recurrence / infection after surgery; (2) technique and minimally-invasive comparisons; (3) adjacent or off-focus but from the same search (case reports, severity measures, etc.) — flagged so the group lead can decide keep/cut. No paper outside these search results is included.

## Group 1 — Directly relevant: recurrence and/or postoperative infection after surgery

1. **PMID: 42633928** — Surgical Treatment Outcomes for Primary Pilonidal Sinus Disease: A Single-Centre Retrospective Study of 2222 Patients (2006-2020). — *International wound journal* — 2026. The centrepiece citation: 2,222 primary PSD patients, nine techniques, minimum 5-year follow-up — 5-year cumulative recurrence 7.9% and postoperative infection 6.9% with both differing significantly across techniques; lowest recurrence with Karydakis flap (1.5%), Bascom cleft lift (2.3%), Dufourmentel flap (2.4%), Limberg flap, and lay-open as reference; most recurrences within 2 years (median time to recurrence 20 months); BMI an independent risk factor — the most empirical long-term dataset returned.

2. **PMID: 42347916** — Outcomes reported in comparative studies of surgical management of pilonidal disease: Systematic review to inform a core outcome set. — *Colorectal disease* — 2026. Directly relevant methodology paper: documents outcome heterogeneity across comparative PSD studies and frames the case for a core outcome set — a well-trodden issue in interpreting any outcome pooled in this review.

3. **PMID: 41854814** — Comparative study of the Karydakis flap versus conventional surgical techniques for pilonidal sinus disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis. — *Updates in surgery* — 2026. Meta-analysis (15 studies, n=3,108, through February 2025) of KF against midline closure and excision healing by secondary intention on recurrence, infection, seroma, operative time, hospital stay, complications and return to work — the most inclusive pooled KF evidence returned, with recurrence significantly reduced vs midline closure.

4. **PMID: 39982788** — Outcome After Surgical Treatment for Chronic Pilonidal Sinus Disease: A Systematic Review of Common Surgical Techniques. — *Diseases of the colon and rectum* — 2025. Synopsis strategies for chronic PSD return; supplies the general technique-level framing that transfusion routes from single-centre data.

5. **PMID: 41074563** — Sacrococcygeal Pilonidal Sinus Disease: A Decade-In-Review of Patient-Reported Outcome Measures. — *ANZ journal of surgery* — 2026. Frames the PROMs decade for residual PSD surgery, capturing how recurrence and surgical site infection rates are documented alongside patient-centred benefit — useful for describing what prior outcome sets measure and what they miss.

6. **PMID: 42703471** — Surgical Management of Pilonidal Sinus: A Comparative Study of Excision, Limberg Flap, and Keystone Flap Reconstruction. — *Cureus* — 2026. Prospective comparative 46-patient cohort (Feb 2024–Jul 2025): 43.5% complication rate led by seroma in flap groups; recurrence 13.3% occurred only in the simple-excision group vs flaps, which performed better on scar quality, satisfaction and QoL — shows off-midline flap reconstruction with longer operative times and stays yielding better recurrence and patient-centred outcomes.

7. **PMID: 42700095** — Comparative outcomes of phenol therapy and Karydakis flap in pilonidal sinus disease: A retrospective cohort study with 1-year follow-up. — *Medicine* — 2026. Retrospective cohort (n=125, Jan 2024–Jan 2025, minimum 1-year follow-up): phenol gave significantly faster wound healing (15.4 vs 36.1 days) and earlier return to work with no significant difference in 1-year recurrence (12.5% vs 9.1%) — defines the minimally invasive?)
