# Surgical Treatment Outcomes in Pilonidal Sinus Disease: First-Pass Literature Notes

**Review focus:** Long-term outcomes after surgery, prioritising **recurrence** and **postoperative infection**.

**Search method:** PubMed database search (run now, not from memory), limited to roughly the last five years (records returned are from 2026).

---

## Articles returned by the live search (exactly as returned)

### 1. PMID 42347916
- **Title:** Outcomes reported in comparative studies of surgical management of pilonidal disease: Systematic review to inform a core outcome set.
- **Journal:** Colorectal Disease (official journal of the Association of Coloproctology of Great Britain and Ireland), 2026
- **Takeaway:** Because evidence synthesis in pilonidal disease surgery is limited by heterogeneity in outcome selection, definition, measurement and follow-up, this systematic review identifies the outcomes reported in contemporary comparative studies to inform development of a core outcome set.

### 2. PMID 42020338
- **Title:** Minimally Invasive, Maximally Effective: Outpatient Strategies for Paediatric Pilonidal Disease.
- **Journal:** International Wound Journal, 2026
- **Takeaway:** A retrospective single-centre cohort of 69 paediatric patients (≤18 years) following a structured tiered outpatient pathway (conservative care, in-clinic debridement, trephination or limited excision) reported recurrence in 26.1% after operative intervention versus 2.2% after non-operative management, though operative patients likely represented a more severe subgroup.

### 3. PMID 41880146
- **Title:** Negative pressure wound therapy in primary closure surgeries for pilonidal disease: a meta-analysis.
- **Journal:** Updates in Surgery, 2026
- **Takeaway:** A meta-analysis of 6 studies (479 patients) found negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) did not significantly improve recurrence (RR 0.54, 95% CI 0.07–4.48) or infection rates (RR 0.38, 95% CI 0.05–2.84) versus control after primary closure, but did shorten time to resume normal activities and reduce 24-hour postoperative pain.

### 4. PMID 41854814
- **Title:** Comparative study of the Karydakis flap versus conventional surgical techniques for pilonidal sinus disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis.
- **Journal:** Updates in Surgery, 2026
- **Takeaway:** Through February 2025 this meta-analysis of 15 studies (n = 3,108) including 1,257 Karydakis flap, 1,593 midline closure and 258 excision-with-healing-by-secondary-intention patients found the Karydakis flap was associated with a significant reduction in recurrence compared to midline closure (OR 0.30, 95% CI 0.16–0.59), with recurrence and infection as primary outcomes.

### 5. PMID 41838186
- **Title:** Surgical preference and outcomes in pilonidal sinus disease: a national multicenter study evaluating complications, recurrence, and quality of life (PISI TURKEY).
- **Journal:** Techniques in Coloproctology, 2026
- **Takeaway:** PISI TURKEY is a national multicentre study of real-world surgical practice for pilonidal sinus disease in Turkey comparing complications, recurrence and quality of life between excisional and minimally invasive procedures.
