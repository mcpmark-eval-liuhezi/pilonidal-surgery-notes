# Pilonidal Sinus Disease: Surgical Outcomes — First-Pass Literature Notes

**Focus:** Long-term outcomes after surgery for pilonidal sinus disease (PSD), with recurrence as the primary outcome of interest and postoperative infection as a key secondary outcome.

**Source:** PubMed database search run for this review (advanced search: "pilonidal sinus disease surgical treatment outcomes recurrence postoperative infection", 2020–2025; plus keyword search on surgical outcomes, recurrence, and infection). All citations below come directly from those search results; nothing has been added from memory.

**Scope note:** The search returned a solid, relevant set of articles from 2024–2026 covering minimally invasive techniques (laser ablation/SiLaT/SiLaC, EPSiT), flap procedures (Karydakis, Limberg, Bascom cleft lift, Dufourmentel), national multicenter studies, and adjunct therapies (negative pressure wound therapy, laser hair removal). No tangential papers were included to pad the list.

---

## Citation List (as returned by the search)

### 1. PMID 42633928 — Surgical Treatment Outcomes for Primary Pilonidal Sinus Disease: A Single-Centre Retrospective Study of 2222 Patients (2006–2020).
**Journal:** International wound journal | **Year:** 2026

**Takeaway:** This large single-centre retrospective cohort of 2,222 primary PSD patients (minimum 5-year follow-up) directly compares long-term recurrence and postoperative infection across nine surgical techniques, reporting a 5-year cumulative recurrence of 7.9% and postoperative infection of 6.9%, with the lowest recurrence rates for Karydakis flap (1.5%), Bascom cleft lift (2.3%), and Dufourmentel flap (2.4%), most recurrences occurring within the first 2 years (median time to recurrence 20 months), and Karydakis, Bascom cleft lift, Limberg, and Dufourmentel flaps independently associated with lower recurrence risk versus lay-open treatment.

### 2. PMID 41278051 — Evaluating Risk Factors for Surgical Site Occurrences: Infection and Wound Dehiscence Post Definitive Surgery for Sacrococcygeal Pilonidal Sinus Disease.
**Journal:** Cureus | **Year:** 2025

**Takeaway:** This study evaluated the rates of surgical site infections (SSIs) and wound dehiscence after definitive surgery for sacrococcygeal pilonidal sinus disease across multiple surgical techniques and identified associated risk factors, making it directly relevant to the postoperative infection focus of this review.

### 3. PMID 41838186 — Surgical preference and outcomes in pilonidal sinus disease: a national multicenter study evaluating complications, recurrence, and quality of life (PISI TURKEY).
**Journal:** Techniques in coloproctology | **Year:** 2026

**Takeaway:** This national multicenter study describes real-world surgical practice for PSD in Turkey and compares complications, recurrence, and quality of life between excision-based and non-excisional (minimally invasive) procedures, providing valuable context on how surgical preference and outcomes vary in a large national cohort.

### 4. PMID 42089833 — Outcomes of Pilonidal Surgery Performed in the Setting of Standard Adjunct Laser Hair Removal: A Retrospective Cohort Study.
**Journal:** International wound journal | **Year:** 2026

**Takeaway:** In a real-world retrospective cohort of 168 patients at Kaiser Permanente Northern California (2012–2024) managed uniformly with adjunctive laser hair removal, the overall repeat procedure rate was 16.7% and the 30-day surgical site infection (SSI) rate was 14.4%, with Bascom flap closure showing fewer repeat procedures (10.4% vs 20.8%) and fewer unplanned clinic visits than pilocystectomy, suggesting postoperative outcomes varied more by surgical approach than by sex or BMI.

### 5. PMID 41880146 — Negative pressure wound therapy in primary closure surgeries for pilonidal disease: a meta-analysis.
**Journal:** Updates in surgery | **Year:** 2026

**Takeaway:** This systematic review and meta-analysis of 6 studies (479 patients) found that negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) after primary closure was associated with lower pain scores at 24 hours postoperatively and shorter return to normal activities, but showed no significant difference in infection rates (RR 0.38, 95% CI 0.05–2.84), recurrence rates (RR 0.54, 95% CI 0.07–4.48), or wound dehiscence, recommending more randomized trials before widespread adoption.

### 6. PMID 42347916 — Outcomes reported in comparative studies of surgical management of pilonidal disease: Systematic review to inform a core outcome set.
**Journal:** Colorectal disease | **Year:** 2026

**Takeaway:** This systematic review identified limited evidence synthesis in pilonidal disease surgery due to heterogeneity in outcome selection, definition, measurement, and follow-up, and aimed to inform development of a core outcome set to improve comparability of future comparative studies.

### 7. PMID 42988951 — Endoscopic pilonidal sinus treatment versus Limberg flap reconstruction for uncomplicated pilonidal sinus disease: a prospective comparative study.
**Journal:** Langenbeck's archives of surgery | **Year:** 2026

**Takeaway:** This prospective comparative study addresses the ongoing controversy in optimal surgical management of sacrococcygeal PSD by comparing minimally invasive endoscopic pilonidal sinus treatment (EPSiT) against the established off-midline Limberg flap, noting that comparative prospective data remain limited.

### 8. PMID 39699772 — Sinus laser therapy versus Karydakis flap procedure in the management of pilonidal sinus disease: a comparative analysis of intraoperative parameters and postoperative outcome.
**Journal:** Techniques in coloproctology | **Year:** 2024

**Takeaway:** This study investigated the utility of minimally invasive sinus laser therapy (SiLaT) versus the Karydakis flap procedure in terms of intraoperative parameters and postoperative outcomes, contributing head-to-head comparative data between a newer minimally invasive option and an established off-midline flap.

### 9. PMID 39140695 — Results of Laser-assisted Closure (SiLaC) Surgery in Pilonidal Sinus Disease: Factors Associated With Success.
**Journal:** Surgical laparoscopy, endoscopy & percutaneous techniques | **Year:** 2024

**Takeaway:** This study examined the efficacy and safety of sinus laser-assisted closure (SiLaC) in a tertiary care training hospital and identified factors associated with success and failure, sharing first experiences with this minimally invasive approach.

### 10. PMID 40522552 — Evaluation of laser ablation for recurrent pilonidal sinus disease: treatment success, recurrence rates, and patient outcomes.
**Journal:** Lasers in medical science | **Year:** 2025

**Takeaway:** This study evaluates the efficacy of laser ablation in patients with recurrent pilonidal sinus disease, focusing on treatment success, recurrence rates, complications, and recovery outcomes, addressing the recurrent-disease subset of the review population.

### 11. PMID 39893840 — Laser Hair Removal May Be a Primary Treatment of Pilonidal Disease: A Pilot Study.
**Journal:** Journal of pediatric surgery | **Year:** 2025

**Takeaway:** This pilot study investigated postoperative laser hair removal (LHR) as a primary treatment strategy for pilonidal disease in adolescents and young adults, where LHR has demonstrated promise to reduce recurrence following surgery but had not yet been studied as a primary approach.

### 12. PMID 39045854 — Treatment options for patients with pilonidal sinus disease: PITSTOP, a mixed-methods evaluation.
**Journal:** Health technology assessment | **Year:** 2024

**Takeaway:** This mixed-methods evaluation highlights that there is no consensus on optimal management of pilonidal disease, with highly varied surgical practice and existing literature consisting mainly of single-centre cohort studies of varied severity, interventions, and outcome assessments.

## Summary of Key Findings for the Review

1. **Off-midline flap techniques (Karydakis, Bascom cleft lift, Limberg, Dufourmentel) showed the lowest long-term recurrence** in the large 2,222-patient cohort (PMID 42633928), with most recurrences occurring within the first 2 years (median 20 months), supporting structured follow-up protocols.
2. **Postoperative infection rates** ranged approximately 6.9% in the large cohort (PMID 42633928) and 14.4% for 30-day SSI in the adjunct LHR cohort (PMID 42089833), with risk factors for SSIs and wound dehiscence identified in PMID 41278051.
3. **Minimally invasive options** (laser ablation/SiLaT/SiLaC, EPSiT) are gaining comparative evidence (PMIDs 39699772, 39140695, 42988951) but long-term recurrence data remain matures than for established flaps.
4. **Adjunct NPWT** showed benefit for pain and return to activities but not for infection or recurrence (PMID 41880146); **adjunct laser hair removal** showed associations with fewer repeat procedures for Bascom flap closure (PMID 42089833).
5. **Outcome heterogeneity** remains a barrier to synthesis, as described in the core outcome set review (PMID 42347916) and the PITSTOP mixed-methods evaluation (PMID 39045854).
